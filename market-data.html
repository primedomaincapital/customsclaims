How to read this page

What the numbers say — and what they don't.

The IEEPA pool is a one-time event; drawback is the permanent business. About $166 billion became refundable when the Supreme Court struck down the IEEPA tariffs on February 20, 2026, and most of the easy claims are already moving through CBP's CAPE process — $136.6 billion accepted by October 2, 2026. What remains is the harder work: finally liquidated entries, importers without ACH bank data on file, and companies waiting on court cases. The permanent opportunity sits beside it: drawback has returned 99 cents on the dollar since 1789, with a five-year lookback, and roughly four-fifths of the money it could return is never claimed.

The protest clock is the constraint that turns paperwork into a product. Liquidation typically lands about 314 days after entry, and the 180-day protest window then runs with no extensions. A firm that watches liquidation dates for its clients owns the timing; a firm that does not watches the refunds expire. That is why the buyers of this name — brokers, recovery firms, customs lawyers — sell vigilance as much as they sell filings.

Figures are dated, not timeless. CAPE totals and collection figures move as CBP processes declarations and the Treasury pays out. The "Last updated" stamp above is the date this page was last checked against its sources; each figure carries the source and date it was taken from.
